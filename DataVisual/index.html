Navient Denies Wrongdoing, but Complaint Data Tells a Different Story

By: Joe Wagman


The Navient logo appears on the screen of a smartphone in Reno, United States, on December 1, 2024. (Photo by Jaque Silva/NurPhoto via Getty Images)

In 2024, Navient faced a lawsuit from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau alleging that the loan servicing unit misled its borrowers into more expensive repayment options. Navient reached a $120 million settlement in September 2024; however, it still denied the allegations made by the CFPB and claimed no wrongdoing. The company said that its reasoning for agreeing to the settlement was to put the issues behind them and move forward.

However, data provided by the CFPB shows that borrower complaints over student loans with Navient were steady each year, while substantially increasing in 2024 from what the number of complaints had been over the four years prior. The company saw almost double the amount of student loan complaints in 2024, with 2,696, as it did in 2020, with 1,459. Most of the complaints over that time period were either dealing with Navient outright or struggles to repay their loans. Specifically looking at issues dealing with Navient, borrowers made 1,920 complaints about receiving bad information about their loans and 1,139 complaints over troubles with how payments were being handled over that five year span.

The main issue borrowers had with Navient was clear. The company received more complaints over borrowers receiving bad information and troubles with how it was handling payments more than any other reported issues. While Navient may not be willing to admit this was a major issue, data provided by the CFPB shows otherwise.
