The Rise and Fall of Chris "Crush" Davis

By Joe Wagman

Davis' Career Path

Chris Davis was drafted three times before finally joining professional baseball. He was originally drafted out of high school in the 50th round of the 2004 MLB draft by the New York Yankees; however, Davis decided to attend Navarro College. Davis was then drafted again in the 35th round of the 2005 MLB Draft by the Los Angeles Angels of Anaheim before joining the Texas Rangers minor league system after being drafted by the team in the fifth round of the 2006 MLB Draft.

Davis made his Major League debut for the Rangers on June 26, 2008. He bounced between the minors and the majors over the next few years before being traded to the Baltimore Orioles on July 30, 2011. He didn’t receive a starting role with Baltimore until 2012, when he would help the ball club reach the playoffs for the first time since 1997.

The first baseman was named to his one and only All-Star team in 2013 in what was the best statistical year of his career. Davis batted under .200 for the first time as a starter in 2014 before leading the league in home runs again in 2015. Davis signed a seven-year, $161 million contract with the Orioles in January 2016 before his career began to steadily decline.

Despite leading the league in home runs in 2015, Davis’ issues with striking out became major as he led the league in strikeouts in both 2015 and 2016. Davis didn’t hit over .200 in his last three MLB seasons from 2018 to 2020 and set a major league record for consecutive at-bats without a hit during that span. After missing the entire 2021 season due to an injury, Davis announced his retirement from baseball on Aug. 12, 2021.
